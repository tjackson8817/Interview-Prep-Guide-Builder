**"I have never officially held a Customer Success title or managed a CS team, only sales titles."**

> "It's true I've never had the CSM title — but for the past two years, I've functionally done the highest-stakes part of this job: I've personally owned renewal conversations on my five largest accounts, because the relationship already ran through me. This framing works because it's honest about the real gap (team leadership) while giving real, verifiable evidence for the part that *is* covered (renewal ownership) — it doesn't try to talk around the management question.

**"I have zero healthcare-industry experience, which the posting flags as preferred."**

> "I don't have healthcare-specific experience, and I won't pretend the learning curve isn't real. What I do bring is two years of operating in a similarly regulated, relationship-heavy environment — financial services accounts, where compliance and stakeholder trust both mattered a great deal. The muscle of navigating complex, high-stakes enterprise relationships transfers; the industry specifics are something I'd need to learn quickly, and I'm confident in my ability to do that based on how fast I ramped in enterprise SaaS."

This one is framed as neutral, not as an advantage — because it genuinely isn't one. The honest version (real transferable muscle, real learning curve, no pretending otherwise) is more credible than forcing a false equivalence between financial services and healthcare.

## 9. Time-Boxing for a 60-Minute Call

A realistic cadence for 60 minutes:

- **0–5 min:** Rapport and introductions.
- **5–45 min:** Their questions — the bulk of the call.
- **45–55 min:** Your questions (Section 12 below).
