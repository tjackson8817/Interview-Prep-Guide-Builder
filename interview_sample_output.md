## 4. Objection Reframing

*Built directly from the two concerns you named — each grounded in something real from your resume, not spin.*

**"I have never officially held a Customer Success title or managed a CS team, only sales titles."**

> "It's true I've never had the CSM title — but for the past two years, I've functionally done the highest-stakes part of this job: I've personally owned renewal conversations on my five largest accounts, because the relationship already ran through me. What I haven't done is manage a team, and I want to be upfront about that — I'd approach the first 90 days by leaning heavily on my CSMs' existing account knowledge while I build my own management muscle, the same disciplined way I built forecast accuracy from scratch when I moved into enterprise accounts."

This framing works because it's honest about the real gap (team leadership) while giving real, verifiable evidence for the part that *is* covered (renewal ownership) — it doesn't try to talk around the management question.

**"I have zero healthcare-industry experience, which the posting flags as preferred."**

> "I don't have healthcare-specific experience, and I won't pretend the learning curve isn't real. What I do bring is two years of operating in a similarly regulated, relationship-heavy environment — financial services accounts, where compliance and stakeholder trust both mattered a great deal. The muscle of navigating complex, high-stakes enterprise relationships transfers; the industry specifics are something I'd need to learn quickly, and I'm confident in my ability to do that based on how fast I ramped in enterprise SaaS."

This one is framed as neutral, not as an advantage — because it genuinely isn't one. The honest version (real transferable muscle, real learning curve, no pretending otherwise) is more credible than forcing a false equivalence between financial services and healthcare.

## 6. Video Call Logistics Checklist

Tied to Microsoft Teams specifically:
- Test your Teams audio/video ahead of time — not just a generic mic check, actually open the app and do a real test call.
- Check your lighting and background on camera.
- Close Slack, email, and any other notification-generating apps before the call starts.

- Keep water nearby.

## 7. Time-Boxing for a 60-Minute Call

A realistic cadence for 60 minutes:
- **0–5 min:** Rapport and introductions.
- **5–45 min:** Their questions — the bulk of the call.
- **45–55 min:** Your questions (Section 10 below).
- **55–60 min:** Wrap-up and next steps.

Keep individual answers to 90 seconds–2 minutes unless you're explicitly asked to go deeper — one long answer can eat a meaningful chunk of a 60-minute call.
